# IV. Structural Reform of Federal Government

Summary

Institutional reforms to improve accountability, reduce polarization, and increase democratic responsiveness through changes to judiciary, legislative structure, and executive-legislative relations.

Policy items

- Supreme Court Terms — Implement fixed, staggered terms for justices and adopt binding judicial ethics standards.
- Judicial Ethics & Oversight — Establish disciplinary mechanisms, transparency rules, and recusal standards for federal judges.
- Legislative Restructuring — Study transition pathways to a unicameral or rebalanced legislature with representation strictly by population.
- Executive Accountability — Strengthen oversight, slow-roll appointment limits, and parliamentary-style confidence mechanisms for executive leadership.
- Nomination & Confirmation Reform — Timelines for Senate action on nominations and reductions in arbitrary holds or filibusters for confirmations.
- Institutional Staffing — Shift professional legislative drafting and research to nonpartisan career staff with public documentation requirements.
- Decentralized Pilot Reforms — Use federal pilot programs for structural changes (e.g., state-level unicameral trials or alternate voting systems).

Implementation & Administration

These reforms will require combinations of federal legislation, constitutional amendments, and state-level experimentation. Key actors include Congress, the judiciary through internal rule changes, and interbranch commissions. Metrics: nomination timelines, public trust indicators, legislative output and quality measures, and frequency of ethics violations. Implementation should prioritize incremental, legally-sound steps and include sunset/pilot provisions to evaluate consequences before wide adoption.

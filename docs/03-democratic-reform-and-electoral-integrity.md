# III. Democratic Reform and Electoral Integrity

Summary

Reforms to reduce the influence of money in politics, improve legislative transparency, and strengthen electoral integrity through public financing and structural safeguards.

Policy items

- Campaign Finance Reform — Establish public financing mechanisms, contribution limits, and disclosure rules to reduce private money influence.
- Citizens United Response — Seek legislative and constitutional pathways to limit corporate election spending and increase disclosure.
- Federally Funded Elections — Pilot and scale matching funds, vouchers, or grants to finance competitive campaigns.
- Lobbying Transparency — Centralize a public registry for lobbyists, disclose meetings and contributions, and limit in-kind campaign support.
- Nonpartisan Drafting — Build a professional legislative drafting office to produce publicly documented bill texts and impact assessments.
- Voting Access & Security — Secure voter registration systems, expand early and mail voting, and standardize ballot design to reduce errors.
- Redistricting Reform — Independent commissions, clear criteria, and public participation to end partisan gerrymandering.
- Electoral System Modernization — Implement nationwide ranked-choice voting and explore proportional representation in pilot jurisdictions.

Implementation & Administration

The Federal Election Commission (or a reformed successor body), the Department of Justice (for enforcement), and state election officials will coordinate implementation. Metrics: campaign spending concentration, number of publicly financed campaigns, turnout rates, incidence of disputed results, and redistricting compliance. Legal change may require federal legislation, incentives to states, and where necessary, constitutional processes for major structural reforms.

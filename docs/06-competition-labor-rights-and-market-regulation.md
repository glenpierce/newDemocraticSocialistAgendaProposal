# VI. Competition, Labor Rights, and Market Regulation

Summary

Policies to restore competitive markets, strengthen labor protections, and rebalance intellectual property to prioritize innovation and public benefit.

Policy items

- Antitrust Enforcement — Expand DOJ and FTC staffing, update merger guidelines, and favor structural remedies where appropriate.
- Breakups & Structural Remedies — Authorize remedial divestitures for dominant firms that stifle competition.
- Labor Rights Expansion — Strengthen collective bargaining rights, streamline union certification, and penalize employer interference.
- Wage & Hour Enforcement — Increase DOL enforcement resources to address wage theft, misclassification, and unpaid overtime.
- Public Procurement Reform — Use government contracting power to promote competition and labor standards.
- IP Reform — Narrow patentability for obvious or overly-broad claims and limit abusive patent assertion entities.
- Platform Regulation — Enforce interoperability, data portability, and nondiscriminatory access for large digital platforms.

Implementation & Administration

The Department of Justice Antitrust Division, the Federal Trade Commission, the Department of Labor, and Congress will play central roles. Metrics: number of antitrust actions, labor union density, wage recovery amounts, and procurement market concentration measures. Implementation will require updated rulemaking, increased appropriations to enforcement agencies, and legislative clarifications to modernize antitrust and labor statutes.

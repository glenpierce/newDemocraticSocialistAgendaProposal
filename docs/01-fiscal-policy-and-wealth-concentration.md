# I. Fiscal Policy and Wealth Concentration

Summary

A fiscal framework to reduce wealth concentration and increase revenue for public investment through progressive taxation, stronger enforcement, and targeted spending. Policies focus on wealth taxation, progressive income tax, strengthened reporting, and mechanisms to direct revenues toward healthcare, education, and infrastructure.

Policy items

- Wealth Taxation — An annual net-asset tax on very high-net-worth households to capture unrealized wealth and reduce extreme concentration.
- Asset Disclosure & Valuation — Mandatory, standardized asset disclosure and valuation rules enforced by a dedicated Treasury unit; close loopholes for trusts and offshore holdings.
- Corporate Tax Reform — Minimum corporate tax, anti-avoidance rules, and tighter transfer pricing enforcement to ensure multinational firms pay their share.
- Capital Gains Reform — Align capital gains rates with ordinary income for top brackets and consider mark-to-market treatment for large portfolios.
- Estate & Gift Tax Modernization — Strengthen estate tax thresholds and anti-avoidance provisions to limit intergenerational wealth concentration.
- Tax Enforcement Capacity — Increase IRS audit funding, data-sharing agreements, and technology for automated compliance targeting.
- Redistribution & Earmarks — Direct new revenue to pre-specified public investments (healthcare, education, infrastructure, research) with transparent budgeting rules.
- Anti-Evasion Measures — Enhance international cooperation, beneficial ownership registries, and penalties for noncompliance.

Implementation & Administration

Primary administration would be by the Department of the Treasury and the Internal Revenue Service, with coordination from the Office of Management and Budget for budgetary routing. Metrics: revenue collected from wealth tax, audit coverage rates, effective tax rates on top percentiles, and capital concentration indices. Implementation steps include drafting statutory language, phased implementation windows, pilot valuation rules, and a dedicated enforcement unit funded through the budget process.

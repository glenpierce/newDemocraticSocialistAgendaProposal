# II. Healthcare System Reform

Summary

A single-payer public healthcare system to ensure universal coverage, comprehensive benefits, and cost control through centralized negotiation and simplified administration.

Policy items

- Single-Payer Coverage — Universal enrollment through a Medicare-for-All-style program covering primary care, hospitals, mental health, long-term care, and prescription drugs.
- Benefits Standardization — Define a comprehensive benefits package and update regularly through an expert advisory board.
- Provider Payment Reform — Move toward value-based payments, bundled payments, and standard fee schedules to control costs and incentivize outcomes.
- Drug Price Negotiation — Centralize negotiation of pharmaceutical prices and allow importation or reference pricing where appropriate.
- Transition Mechanisms — Phased transition from employer-based coverage, with timelines, buyouts, or conversions for existing plans and providers.
- Health Workforce Planning — Federal investment in training, recruitment, and distribution of primary care and mental health professionals.
- Administrative Simplification — Consolidate billing and claims systems, reduce overhead, and invest in interoperable health IT.
- Financing Framework — Dedicated revenue sources (progressive taxes, payroll adjustments, and reallocation of current public spending) and fiscal sustainability rules.

Implementation & Administration

The Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) would lead program design and implementation in coordination with HHS, SSA (for eligibility), and state partners for service delivery. Key metrics: coverage rate, per-capita health spending trends, wait times for core services, and out-of-pocket spending. Implementation will proceed in phases with pilot regions, federal-state agreements, provider payment pilots, and statutory financing measures enacted to secure funding and legal authority.

# XI. Science, Technology, and Public Ownership

Summary

A policy agenda to expand public investment in research and ensure publicly funded technology delivers public benefit through licensing, open access, and responsible commercialization.

Policy items

- Increased R&D Funding — Scale up federal research budgets at NSF, NIH, DOE, and ARPA-style agencies for basic and applied research.
- Public Licensing Requirements — Require non-exclusive licensing or public-interest licensing terms for technologies developed with federal funds.
- Open Science — Mandate open-access publication and data-sharing for publicly funded research where privacy/security allows.
- Public Manufacturing Partnerships — Use public procurement and investment to support domestic manufacturing of technologies developed with public funds.
- Ethical & Social Oversight — Establish technology impact assessments and ethics review for high-risk emerging tech (AI, biotech).
- Technology Transfer Reform — Rebalance university-industry transfer incentives to prioritize public benefit and affordability.

Implementation & Administration

Agencies: NSF, NIH, DOE, the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy, and the National Science Foundation for coordination. Metrics: share of federally funded tech under non-exclusive licenses, R&D spending as % of GDP, reproducibility/compliance rates for open science, and domestic manufacturing capacity for key technologies. Implementation will require grant-condition changes, procurement policy updates, and interagency coordination on licensing and IP terms.
